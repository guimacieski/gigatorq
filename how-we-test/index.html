Our method

How we test

Every GigaTorq comparison follows the same rules, so results are fair and repeatable.

Where our tools come from

We buy most tools at retail. When a manufacturer loans or gives us a tool, we say so in the review. Brands never see a review before it's published and can't pay for a better score.

Same jobs, same materials

  • Drills: spade bits and auger bits in 2x10 SPF lumber, twist bits in mild steel.
  • Impact drivers: 3-in deck screws and 1/2-in x 6-in lag screws in pressure-treated lumber.
  • Saws: rip and cross cuts in 2x and 3/4-in plywood, cuts per charge.
  • Grinders: cutting rebar and grinding mild steel, timed.

Batteries start fully charged, and we use the battery size that comes in the most common kit.

How we score

Each tool gets a score out of 10 in four areas:

  • Power - speed and torque in real tasks, not just spec-sheet numbers.
  • Runtime - work done per charge and recharge time.
  • Ergonomics - weight, balance, grip, vibration and controls.
  • Value - performance for the price, plus warranty and battery platform.

Keeping reviews current

Prices and models change. We update comparisons when a new model replaces an old one, and every page shows when it was last updated.
