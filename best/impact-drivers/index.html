Best overall

Milwaukee 2953

2,000 in-lbs and 4,400 impacts per minute in a 4.47-inch body.

2,000 in-lbs1,400 / 3,600 / 4,400 IPM4.47 inM18Brushless

Milwaukee rates the M18 FUEL 2953 at 2,000 in-lbs and up to 4,400 impacts per minute, with three speeds up to 3,900 RPM and four drive modes. At 4.47 inches long and 2.2 lb without a battery, it's one of the shortest 18V-class drivers we track, and it has a 5-year tool warranty.

Best for
Pros and serious DIYers who drive a lot of long screws and lag bolts, or already own M18 batteries.
Skip it if
You mostly assemble furniture and hang shelves. A 12V driver is lighter and plenty.

Most power

Flex FX1371A

Tied for the most torque we track, with the highest top speed.

2,500 in-lbs4,450 IPM4.7 in24VBrushless

Flex rates the FX1371A at 2,500 in-lbs, tied with DeWalt's DCF860 for the most torque of any impact driver in our database. Its top speed of 4,000 RPM is the highest we track, and it hits 4,450 impacts per minute in a 4.7-inch body. The tool warranty is 5 years if you register it.

Best for
Heavy fastening like long lag screws, structural screws and deck framing, especially on Flex 24V batteries.
Skip it if
Weight matters most. At 2.7 lb without a battery, it has the highest tool-only weight of our picks.

Best lightweight

Milwaukee 3453

The lightest driver we track, at 1.6 lb without a battery.

1,500 in-lbs4,000 IPM5 inM12Brushless

The M12 FUEL 3453 weighs 1.6 lb bare and is 5 inches long, yet Milwaukee rates it at 1,500 in-lbs and 4,000 impacts per minute, with three speeds up to 3,600 RPM. That's more torque than some 18V drivers on this list.

Best for
Overhead work, cabinets and trim, and long days of light to medium fastening.
Skip it if
You drive big lag bolts every day. An 18V driver with 2,000 in-lbs or more does heavy work faster.

2,200 in-lbs from Ryobi's brushless ONE+ HP line.

2,200 in-lbs4,000 IPM18V ONE+Brushless

Ryobi rates the ONE+ HP PBLID02 at 2,200 in-lbs and 4,000 impacts per minute, with four drive modes. On paper that's more torque than the Milwaukee 2953, but its top speed is lower at 2,900 RPM. It weighs 2.55 lb without a battery and runs on any Ryobi 18V ONE+ battery.

Best for
Owners of Ryobi 18V ONE+ batteries who want brushless power.
Skip it if
You're not on Ryobi batteries, or you want to compare body length. We couldn't find a published length for it.

Makita XDT19

Four speed settings in a 4.56-inch body for Makita 18V LXT owners.

1,590 in-lbs1,100 / 2,600 / 3,600 / 3,800 IPM4.56 in18V LXTBrushless

The XDT19 has four speeds (1,100 to 3,600 RPM) and up to 3,800 impacts per minute, which gives fine control with small screws and full power for bigger ones. Makita rates it at 1,590 in-lbs and lists it at 3.3 lb with a battery and 4.56 inches long.

Best for
Makita 18V LXT owners who want a compact driver with fine speed control.
Skip it if
You want the most torque. At 1,590 in-lbs, it's behind the 2,000 in-lb drivers on this page.

Questions

How should I choose an impact driver?

Torque. Impact drivers are rated in inch-pounds (in-lbs). 1,500 in-lbs drives most deck and cabinet screws. Long lag screws and structural screws go faster at 2,000 in-lbs and up.

Impact rate and speed. Impacts per minute (IPM) and RPM tell you how fast the driver works. Several speed settings or drive modes help you avoid stripping or snapping small screws.

Size and weight. Short bodies fit between studs and inside cabinets. Brands usually publish weight without the battery, so compare tool-only weights with tool-only weights.

12V or 18V. 12V drivers are lighter and smaller and handle most household jobs. 18V-class drivers have more torque for heavy fastening.

Battery platform. The battery is the expensive part. If you already own batteries from one brand, a bare tool from that brand usually saves the most money.

Impact driver or drill?

A drill makes clean holes and controls screws with a clutch. An impact driver drives long screws and lag bolts more easily and with less strain on your wrist. Many people buy both, which is why they're often sold together as combo kits.

Can I use drill bits in an impact driver?

Only bits with a 1/4-inch hex shank, since an impact driver has a hex collet instead of a chuck. For precise holes, a drill is still the better tool.

Impact driver or impact wrench?

An impact driver takes hex bits for screws. An impact wrench has a square drive for sockets and is made for nuts and bolts, like lug nuts.

Is more torque always better?

No. Very high torque can snap small screws and strip heads. Look for speed settings or drive modes if you also do light work.
