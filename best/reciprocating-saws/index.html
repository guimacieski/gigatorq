Questions

How should I choose a reciprocating saw?

Stroke length. A longer stroke (1-1/8 to 1-1/4 in.) removes more material with each pass. Compact one-handed saws usually have shorter strokes, around 7/8 in.

Strokes per minute. SPM is how fast the blade moves. Higher is faster in wood. For metal, a lower speed makes blades last longer.

Orbital action. Orbital mode moves the blade in a slight loop, which cuts wood faster. Turn it off for metal and cleaner cuts.

Full-size or one-handed. Full-size saws are for demolition. One-handed saws are lighter and better for pruning, plumbing and overhead cuts.

Blades. Use the right blade: coarse teeth for wood and demolition, fine teeth for metal, and bi-metal blades for nail-embedded wood.

What is a reciprocating saw used for?

Demolition, cutting through walls and floors, cutting pipe, pruning branches and any rough cut where speed matters more than a clean edge.

Corded or cordless?

Cordless saws handle most work. Corded saws are still a good choice for long demolition days near an outlet.

Can it cut metal?

Yes, with a metal-cutting blade and a lower speed, without orbital action.

Is a one-handed saw enough?

For pruning, PVC and occasional cuts, yes. For demolition, a full-size saw is far faster.
