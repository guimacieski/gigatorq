The M18 FUEL 2834 spins its 7-1/4 in. blade at 6,000 RPM and cuts 2-5/8 in. deep at 90 degrees and 2 in. at 45, tied for the deepest cut in our database. At 7.9 lb without a battery, it's the lightest 7-1/4 in. saw with a published tool-only weight, and it bevels to 57 degrees. The tool warranty is 5 years.

Best for
Contractors and DIYers who cut a lot of framing lumber and plywood, or already own M18 batteries.
Skip it if
You only make a few cuts a year. A corded saw does the same work for less.

Questions

How should I choose a circular saw?

Blade size. 7-1/4 in. is the standard for framing and cuts through a 2x in one pass. 6-1/2 in. saws are smaller and lighter, but cut less deep.

Cut depth. Look for 2-1/2 in. or more at 90 degrees for framing lumber. The depth at 45 degrees matters for bevel cuts.

Sidewinder or rear-handle. Sidewinders have the motor beside the blade and are shorter and lighter. Rear-handle saws have the motor behind the blade and are preferred by many framers for long, straight cuts.

Corded or cordless. Corded saws are lighter and cheaper and never run out of charge. Cordless saws go anywhere, but need large batteries for heavy cutting.

Brake and bevel. An electric brake stops the blade in a second or two. A bevel that goes past 45 degrees helps with roof and stair cuts.

What size circular saw should I buy?

A 7-1/4 in. saw for most people. It's the standard size and blades are everywhere.

Can a cordless circular saw replace a corded one?

For most work, yes, with a large battery. For all-day ripping, a corded saw is still lighter and never stops to charge.

What blade should I use?

A 24-tooth framing blade for lumber and fast cuts, and a 40- to 60-tooth blade for clean cuts in plywood.

Is an electric brake worth it?

It's a nice safety feature: the blade stops quickly when you let go of the trigger, so you can set the saw down sooner.
