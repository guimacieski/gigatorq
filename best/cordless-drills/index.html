Questions

How should I choose a drill/driver?

Torque. Torque (in-lbs) tells you how hard a drill can twist before it stalls. 300 to 500 in-lbs covers furniture, shelves and pilot holes. Large hole saws, spade bits and lag screws call for 700 in-lbs and up. Some brands, including DeWalt and Craftsman, publish "unit watts out" instead, which can't be compared across brands.

Voltage. "20V MAX" and "18V" batteries are the same thing: 20V is the voltage with no load, 18V is the nominal voltage under load. Our table shows the nominal number so you can compare like for like.

Brushless or brushed. Brushless motors run cooler, last longer and get more out of each charge. A brushed drill like the Ryobi PCL206 is fine for light use and costs less.

Weight. Brands usually publish weight without the battery, and a battery adds roughly 1 to 1.5 lb depending on its size. Compare tool-only weights with tool-only weights.

Battery platform. The battery is the expensive part. If you already own batteries from one brand, buying a bare tool from that brand usually saves the most money.

Is a 20V MAX drill more powerful than an 18V drill?

No, not because of the voltage. 20V MAX batteries are 18V nominal, the same as 18V batteries. Motor, gearing and battery size make the difference.

Do I need a hammer drill?

Only for drilling into brick, block or concrete. For wood, metal and drywall, a drill/driver is lighter and does the same job.

Drill or impact driver?

A drill makes clean holes and controls screws with its clutch. An impact driver drives long screws and lag bolts more easily. Many people buy both, which is why so many are sold as 2-tool combo kits.

Should I buy a kit or a bare tool?

If you don't own the brand's batteries, buy a kit. The battery and charger are worth far more bought together. If you do own them, the bare tool is the better deal.
