Best Cordless Drills

Updated October 24, 2026 · By the GigaTorq team · Research-based, not hands-on tested

A drill/driver is the first cordless tool most people buy, and the one they use most: drilling holes in wood and metal, driving screws, mixing paint and running hole saws. We went through the published specs of 20 drill/drivers from 12 brands, checked current prices and owner ratings at a major US retailer, and picked the six that make the most sense for different buyers.

We didn't test these drills ourselves. We use AI tools to gather and organize the research, and every number on this page comes from the brand or a major retailer, linked in the Sources section. Here's how we choose.

Best overall

Milwaukee 2903

M18 FUEL 1/2" Drill/Driver

The most torque in our group, in a body under 7 inches long.

Milwaukee rates the M18 FUEL 2903 at 1,400 in-lbs, tied with Flex's FX1171T for the highest torque of any drill/driver in our database, and it does that at 6.9 inches long and 3.3 lb without a battery.

That headroom matters for big hole saws and self-feed bits: Milwaukee lists capacity for 3-1/2 in. hole saws and 2-9/16 in. self-feed bits in wood. It also gets the longest tool warranty of our picks, 5 years.

Best for

Pros, remodelers and anyone who drills large holes or already owns M18 batteries.

Skip it if

You mostly hang shelves and assemble furniture. You'd be paying for power you won't use.

Best compact

DeWalt DCD800

20V MAX XR 1/2-in Brushless Cordless Drill/Driver

DeWalt's short, brushless drill for 20V MAX owners.

At 6.37 inches front to back, the DCD800 is one of the shortest full-size 1/2 in. drills we compared, which helps between studs and joists. It has a metal ratcheting chuck, 650 and 2,000 RPM speeds and a 3-mode LED.

DeWalt doesn't publish torque for this drill. It only says it delivers up to 40% more power (unit watts out) than the older DCD791, so we can't rank it on torque.

Best for

How to choose a cordless drill

Torque

Torque (in-lbs) tells you how hard a drill can twist before it stalls. 300 to 500 in-lbs covers furniture, shelves and pilot holes. Large hole saws, spade bits and lag screws call for 700 in-lbs and up. Some brands, including DeWalt and Craftsman, publish "unit watts out" instead, which can't be compared across brands.

Voltage

"20V MAX" and "18V" batteries are the same thing: 20V is the voltage with no load, 18V is the nominal voltage under load. Our table shows the nominal number so you can compare like for like.

Brushless or brushed

Brushless motors run cooler, last longer and get more out of each charge. A brushed drill like the Ryobi PCL206 is fine for light use and costs less.

Weight

Brands usually publish weight without the battery, and a battery adds roughly 1 to 1.5 lb depending on its size. Compare tool-only weights with tool-only weights.

Battery platform

The battery is the expensive part. If you already own batteries from one brand, buying a bare tool from that brand usually saves the most money.

FAQ

Is a 20V MAX drill more powerful than an 18V drill?

No, not because of the voltage. 20V MAX batteries are 18V nominal, the same as 18V batteries. Motor, gearing and battery size make the difference.

Do I need a hammer drill?

Only for drilling into brick, block or concrete. For wood, metal and drywall, a drill/driver is lighter and does the same job.

Drill or impact driver?

A drill makes clean holes and controls screws with its clutch. An impact driver drives long screws and lag bolts more easily. Many people buy both, which is why so many are sold as 2-tool combo kits.

Should I buy a kit or a bare tool?

If you don't own the brand's batteries, buy a kit. The battery and charger are worth far more bought together. If you do own them, the bare tool is the better deal.
