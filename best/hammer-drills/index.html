Milwaukee rates the M18 FUEL 2904 at 1,400 in-lbs and 33,000 blows per minute, the second-highest hammer rate we track. It's 6.9 inches long and 3.3 lb without a battery, with a ratcheting keyless chuck, 5/8 in. masonry capacity and room for 3-1/2 in. hole saws in wood. The tool warranty is 5 years.

Pros and remodelers who drill into masonry now and then and need a strong drill the rest of the time.

Questions

How should I choose a hammer drill?

Hammer mode. In hammer mode the chuck taps forward thousands of times a minute while it spins, which chips through brick and block. Switch it off for wood and metal and it works like a regular drill.

Blows per minute. BPM is how fast the hammer works. Higher is faster in masonry, but a hammer drill is still much slower than a rotary hammer in hard concrete.

Torque. Torque matters for big hole saws, spade bits and augers in wood. 700 in-lbs and up handles most heavy drilling.

Masonry capacity. Brands list the largest hole they recommend in masonry, usually 3/8 to 5/8 in. For bigger holes or lots of concrete, use an SDS rotary hammer.

Weight. A hammer drill is a little heavier than a drill/driver. If you rarely drill masonry, a plain drill/driver is lighter.

Hammer drill or regular drill?

Only buy a hammer drill if you drill into brick, block or concrete. For wood, metal and drywall, a drill/driver does the same job and is lighter.

Hammer drill or rotary hammer?

A hammer drill handles occasional small holes in masonry. A rotary hammer uses SDS bits and a piston mechanism that hits much harder, so it's far faster in concrete.

Can I use a hammer drill as a regular drill?

Yes. Turn the mode collar to drill or drive mode and the hammer action stops.

What bits do I need for masonry?

Carbide-tipped masonry bits. Regular twist bits dull quickly in brick and concrete.
