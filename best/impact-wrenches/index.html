Questions

How should I choose an impact wrench?

Breakaway vs fastening torque. Breakaway (or nut-busting) torque is how hard the wrench can work to loosen a stuck bolt. Fastening torque is what it delivers when tightening. Breakaway numbers are always higher.

Drive size. 3/8-inch drives suit small bolts and tight spaces, 1/2-inch is the common size for cars and general work, and 3/4-inch is for trucks and heavy equipment.

Mid-torque or high-torque. Mid-torque wrenches (around 500 to 650 ft-lbs) are smaller and handle most car work. High-torque models (1,000 ft-lbs and up) are for rusted, large or heavily torqued bolts.

Anvil type. A friction ring holds sockets in place and lets you change them quickly. A pin or hog-ring anvil holds them more securely.

Weight. High-torque wrenches weigh 5 to 6.5 lb without a battery. Mid-torque models are lighter and easier to use overhead.

Can I tighten lug nuts with an impact wrench?

Snug them with the wrench on a low setting, then finish with a torque wrench to your vehicle's spec. A powerful impact wrench can easily over-tighten lug nuts.

Impact wrench or impact driver?

An impact wrench has a square drive for sockets and much more torque. An impact driver takes hex bits for screws.

Do I need impact sockets?

Yes. Use impact-rated sockets. Regular chrome sockets can crack under impact.

How much torque do I need for car work?

Most car lug nuts are torqued well under 200 ft-lbs, so a mid-torque wrench handles routine work. Rusted suspension and exhaust bolts are where high-torque models help.
